METHOD / LIMITS / PROVENANCE

What the numbers mean

An exploratory heuristic, not a validated aviation forecast. The web conversion preserves the original scoring functions for complete data. It does not establish that those functions predict flyability or safety.

First the veto, then the score

The engine checks the configured wind sector, rounded 10 m and 925 hPa wind, rounded gusts, precipitation, snowfall, low cloud and north–south pressure gradient. A veto removes the final fly score, even when the potential is high. All veto reasons are available in hourly evidence.

Three positive components, two penalties

Potential = 0.25 × launch + 0.35 × upper wind + 0.40 × thermal

Fly score = clamp(potential − risk penalty − foehn penalty, 0, 100)

Launch
Wind direction, speed, gust spread and low cloud.
Upper wind
Wind at 925, 850, 800 and 700 hPa, with penalties for strong flow and changes between levels.
Thermal
Solar radiation, sunshine duration, cloud, temperature–dewpoint spread and CAPE, reduced by surface wind and low-level shear.
Risk
Higher is worse. Convective proxies, precipitation, humidity, gust strength and hourly wind increases. Its penalty is 20% of the risk score.
Foehn
Higher is worse. Pressure gradient, northerly winds and vertical wind structure. The adjusted score produces a penalty from 0 to 25 points.

The daily label

The engine considers hourly samples from 06:00 to 20:00 local time. Samples without a veto and with fly score ≥ 35 are grouped into consecutive windows. The best window balances mean score, duration and potential. Labels range from “bad idea” through “if you must”, “meh”, “not bad” to “hammer day”. These are the original engine’s names, not flight recommendations.

Window duration counts samples: 10:00–12:00 contains three hourly samples, not three elapsed hours between those labels. The summary is for the full day, not just the hours remaining. It does not exclude darkness; sunrise and sunset are shown separately.

Regime icon key

Icons describe the model’s regime or window; faces retain the overall verdict. Hover, focus or tap an icon to read its meaning. A thermal regime does not override a veto. Missing data and inactive thermals have different symbols.

The cloudbase axis

The T−Td panel has a left axis in °C and a right axis in metres above the model surface (AGL). Its dashed cloudbase estimate uses 125 × max(T − Td, 0). Because the axes are proportional, the curves overlap when the spread is positive: these are two presentations of the same input, not independent evidence. Hover shows the two units explicitly. This estimate needs temperature and dew point, even when another missing input prevents an overall score.

Missing is not calm

The original engine substitutes defaults for missing data. This preview instead withholds all scores for an hour missing a scoring input, including the previous-hour wind values needed by trend calculations. If any of the 15 daytime samples is incomplete, the daily verdict and best window are withheld. Optional diagnostics can be missing without invalidating a score. Charts keep gaps and never connect across them.

Data and time

Forecast fields come from the selected Open-Meteo model. Reference pressures always come from ICON EU, using the configured north and south locations. The requested horizon is five days; some models provide less coverage or fewer variables. No model fallback is performed silently.

“Retrieved” is the time this server downloaded the data, not the model run or issuance time. Results are cached in memory for 30 minutes per site/model. Reopening a page within that period reuses the same result. An upstream failure is displayed as an error; expired data is never shown as fresh. Process health at /healthz does not imply that weather data is available.

Limits worth keeping in view

  • 10 m wind describes the model grid, not an observation at the launch. Local slope winds, rotor, obstacles and terrain are not resolved reliably by this score.
  • Pressure levels are not fixed heights above the launch and can lie below local terrain. Their approximate sea-level altitudes are not a site-specific vertical profile.
  • Cloudbase is the rough approximation 125 × (T − Td), in metres above the model surface. It is not an observed cloud ceiling or an AMSL altitude.
  • The inherited model reduces foehn influence when thermal scores rise. That interaction is an unvalidated heuristic, not evidence that thermals make foehn safe. The pressure-gradient veto remains unchanged.
  • CAPE, cloud cover and humidity are proxies, not a reliable storm-warning service. The score does not use lightning potential, even when that optional field is returned.
  • The old advisor legend reverses the risk direction, and the original manual’s final interpretation table reverses risk and foehn. This interface follows the code: higher risk and foehn scores are worse. The AI advisor is not connected.

What this lab version leaves out

No AI calls, saved profiles, site editing, standalone login, or production deployment. The preview is protected by the lab’s external login and is not designed for direct public exposure. No personal records are stored; the only browser preference is the colour theme.
